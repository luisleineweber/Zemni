# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Students working with lecture slides, scripts, and other study documents, especially learners preparing for exams or the German Abitur. They use Zemni while turning a long PDF or Markdown document into something they can review, practice, export, and revisit later.

## Product Purpose

Zemni converts study documents into exam-oriented summaries, flashcards, and quizzes. Its core job is to reduce the time between receiving source material and having a usable study session, while keeping the output grounded in the source and transparent enough to inspect. Success means a learner can upload a document, choose the learning mode and model, generate material, refine or study it, and find it again without losing context.

## Brand Personality

Clear, focused, quietly confident. The product should feel like a capable study desk: calm enough for concentrated work, direct enough to keep the learner moving, and specific about what the system is doing. Copy should be concise, exam-oriented, and avoid generic AI theatrics or inflated promises.

## Anti-references

Avoid the visual and interaction patterns of generic AI SaaS dashboards: gradient-heavy decoration, excessive cards, noisy metric panels, vague marketing language, hidden system state, and feature density that competes with the study task. Avoid making the product feel like a general chatbot when the workflow is document-to-study-material. Do not hide important generation, cost, error, or export states behind subtle icons or delayed feedback.

## Design Principles

1. Put the study task before the tool: upload, generate, inspect, and practice should remain the dominant path.
2. Show provenance and system state: learners should understand what was generated, from which source, with which model, and whether work is cached, pending, failed, or saved.
3. Prefer progressive disclosure: keep the first generation path short, then expose model, structure, export, integration, and advanced settings when they become relevant.
4. Support return visits: history, folders, saved outputs, and exports should preserve the learner's context instead of treating each generation as disposable.
5. Treat AI output as study material, not authority: make inspection, correction, refinement, and source comparison easy.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the core workflow. Preserve keyboard access, visible focus, semantic labels, readable contrast, screen-reader announcements for asynchronous generation and save states, and reduced-motion behavior. Keep touch targets usable on small screens and avoid making color the only signal for status, subscription access, or errors. Support dark mode without reducing legibility.
